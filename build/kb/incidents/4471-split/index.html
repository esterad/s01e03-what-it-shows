#4471, split: three problems, three tickets

owner esterad open reviewed 2026-11-11 · 0 days ago

#4471 is marked known: one issue, one fix queued. Read what the reports show, and where each date came in, and it is three problems. The queued parser fix closes the biggest one. The other two stay broken after it ships. Each problem now has its own ticket, with the number of reports it covers.

#4471 is not one bug. Fixing the parser leaves the booking-v3 form and the one-day shift broken.

The counts are computed at render time from reports-4471.json, the tenant directory and desk-notes.json. A report that appears twice in the export counts once. Each report is placed by what its body shows, its tenant, its channel and its tenant’s booking form. Never by its subject line or its intake tag.

The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first (issue 1). Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a booking for 7 April becomes 4 July. It comes in through every channel at every tenant. This is the fix that is already queued.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type month first (issue 2). Its date field prints MM/DD/YYYY, then reads the field the tenant’s way, day first. People do what the hint says and the booking lands swapped. Only reports sent through the form of a booking-v3 tenant show it. The swap reads the same as the parser’s, but it happens before the parser, so the parser fix does not reach it. Fix the hint, or read the field the way the hint asks.

shift: the date moves by exactly one day (issue 3). There is no swap: Tuesday becomes Monday, one meeting is held twice for Lisbon and Toronto, an invite arrives before its booking. That is a timezone or DST conversion, not the parser. The desk had already read these as a one-day move, but the #4471 table still files them under parser by tenant, so they had no ticket.

Why the merge hid them

  • Triage merges on the subject line (decision 0001). All three problems arrive under the same subjects (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Date is wrong”), so subject matching cannot tell them apart.
  • Intake tags a report from the reporter’s browser locale. The cause depends on the tenant’s locale and booking form, so the tag says hint for parser reports and parser for hint reports.
  • The export repeats some rows across its pages. Count reports by id, not rows.
